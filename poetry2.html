Today's Poetry

Day 2.

Blood is an easy word, a simple tether that ties names on paper, but kinship to me, is not merely who you are born to, it is who my soul recognizes as home. When I think of kinship, I think of the threads, woven, tangled, frayed, yet unbroken, threads that bind us across distance, across silence, threads that cannot be cut even by grief. You are my echo, my mirror, and though our lives branch in directions unalike, the roots beneath are the same, drawing from the same soil, drinking the same rain. Even in our numerous moments of absence, when my voice falters and my hands cannot reach, there is a familiarity that never wanes. It is the warmth in a crowded room, the recognition in a single glance, the comfort of knowing that even when I am shattered, I am not alone. Kinship is the town that never burns, the garden that never fully withers, because its roots go too deep, far beyond time, far beyond circumstance. And when I speak of kinship, I speak of you, the one whose very existence reminds me that I belong, that I am tethered, that I am whole.
